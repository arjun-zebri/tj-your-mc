# Project brief

## The client

TJ, wedding and event MC, Sydney. Existing paid Zebri user. Current site is
`tjyourmc.com.au`, a WordPress and Elementor build by an offshore agency, live
since roughly August 2025.

TJ's brief in his own words was to do whatever we want. That is trust, not a
scope, so this document is the scope.

## Commercials

TJ is paying the standard package rate. We are deliberately overdelivering to a
premium standard because this becomes the flagship case study for Knotify's
website service, which sells alongside Zebri.

That means the bar is not "worth what he paid". The bar is "a site we would put
at the top of a portfolio page and quote five thousand dollars against".

Domain stays with TJ. Knotify manages hosting and maintenance on an ongoing
monthly fee. If TJ ever leaves, he gets the repo and points the domain elsewhere.
Worth writing into the maintenance agreement so it never becomes an argument.

## Timeline

**Week one.** Core five pages, design, copy, form wired to Zebri, redirects,
launch.

**Week two.** SEO and AEO content pages: MC versus celebrant, cost, and
cultural pages if TJ confirms them. Corporate is out, see the decision log.

Split this way because trying to write the content pages in week one means
writing them badly, and the cultural pages are blocked on TJ regardless.

## What is wrong with the current site

Worth stating plainly, because it is the list we are measured against.

1. Keyword-stuffed agency copy. "Professional MC in Sydney" appears three times
   in one homepage paragraph.
2. A factual error. The about page says "I'm not just any civil celebrant". TJ is
   not a celebrant.
3. No audio and no video. He sells a voice, and you cannot hear it anywhere.
4. Testimonials with no venue, no date, no photo, one with no surname.
5. Contact details are the word "Australia" and a gmail address. No phone.
6. No pricing, no packages, no sense of what booking involves.
7. Enquiry form with no visible success or error state.
8. `og:locale` set to `en_US`. Open Graph image is a 372x488 portrait that crops
   badly in every share preview.
9. Duplicate homepage served at both `/` and `/home/`.
10. Voice slides between "I" and "our" inside the same section.

## What success looks like

Ranked, so tradeoffs are obvious.

1. **Enquiries land in Zebri.** A beautiful site that drops leads into gmail is
   worse than the one we replaced, because TJ has to rekey everything.
2. **A couple can hear TJ within fifteen seconds of arriving.** This is the
   single biggest gap in the category and the core of the design.
3. **Rankings hold through the migration.** Two to four weeks of dip is normal.
   Losing the position permanently is not.
4. **It does not look like the other Sydney MC sites.** If it reads as a
   template, the case study is worthless.
5. **Answer engines cite it.** Nobody in this niche has written a decent answer
   to "what does a wedding MC actually do". We can own that cheaply.

## Out of scope for now

- A CMS. Content lives in typed files. Revisit if TJ wants to self-edit.
- Online booking or payments. The pipeline is a conversation, not a checkout.
- A blog. Landing pages first, they earn more per hour of writing.
- Corporate work of any kind. The page was built and then removed on
  2 September 2026. Weddings pay the bills.
